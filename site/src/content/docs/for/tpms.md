You run the program but manage none of the people. Every outcome you own routes through teams that don't report to you, priorities you don't set, and a weekly status update that half your stakeholders skim and the other half misread. The work that makes programs succeed — unblocking, aligning, chasing the orphaned problem nobody owns — is precisely the work that's hardest to point at come review time. Being essential and being seen are, frustratingly, different problems.

bettersense treats both as real.

## Your starter kit

Five things. Start here.

| | When it fires | What it does for you |
|---|---|---|
| `the-program-manager` | Running anything multi-team, multi-month | Dependency mapping, risk gates per phase, go/no-go criteria, escalation framing — program mechanics as a discipline, not vibes. |
| `influence-without-authority` | "I need Platform to prioritize this and I'm not their boss" | Coalition-building, positions vs. interests, sequencing the asks — opinionated about the line between influence and manipulation. |
| `the-status-crafter` | The weekly/monthly update | Status that provides signal: honest RAG with evidence, decisions explicitly requested, risks actually named. Catches watermelon status before your stakeholders do. |
| `stakeholder-reflect` | After the steering meeting | Your program lives on relationships with people you don't manage. Dated reflections on the skeptical principal, the checked-out sponsor, the ally you're under-using. |
| `weekly` | Friday, 15 minutes | Wins captured (including the invisible unblocking work), one overdue stakeholder reflected on, cross-program patterns surfaced. |

## A week with bettersense

**Monday.** An issue has been bouncing between two teams for three weeks; both think the other owns it. `the-program-manager` helps you adopt it formally — owner, decision needed, date — and frame the escalation so it reads as clarity, not blame.

**Wednesday.** You need the data platform team to move your migration up a quarter. `influence-without-authority` maps who actually influences their roadmap, what *their* problem is, and which order to have the conversations in. The answer is not "email their manager."

**Friday, 2pm.** `the-status-crafter` assembles the weekly update — and flags that you've reported zero risks three weeks running on a hard program, which reads as either luck or silence. You add the real one. At 3pm, `/bettersense:weekly`: the two-team unblock gets logged as a win with named evidence, because in six months nobody will remember it happened — including you.

## When you're ready for more

- **The glue problem:** `glue-audit` quantifies the invisible coordination work you do; `wins-curate` turns the log into a review-ready case that frames it as leadership, not overhead.
- **Reading the room:** `read-the-room` for the steering meeting where everyone nodded and nothing was agreed.
- **Risk discipline:** `premortem` before every major phase gate — solo or facilitated with the team.
- **First-time setup:** run `/bettersense:start` — profile, one stakeholder, this kit.
